## To the Football Association

We are writing as an independent project, not a representative body, and we
want to be clear about what this letter is and is not. It is not a demand
for anyone's removal. It does not blame any individual player, manager or
technical director for a result. It does not treat any single tournament
exit as proof of a broader failure. It does not ask the FA to copy Spain, or
any other nation's model, wholesale. And it does not assume that the
Premier League's global commercial success automatically translates into
international performance — we do not believe that, and we would not ask
you to act as though we did.

## What we think you have built well

St George's Park has given England's age-group teams, senior team and coach
education a shared national home since it opened in 2012 — a genuine
institutional achievement that most footballing nations do not have. England
DNA represented a serious attempt to give England's development pathway a
common reference point where none had existed before. England's age-group
teams have had a strong run of results in the years since, including
under-17 and under-20 World Cup wins in 2017, alongside senior tournament
performances — reaching a European Championship final, a World Cup
semi-final, and further deep tournament runs — that mark a real improvement
on the two decades before St George's Park opened. We say this because it is
true, not as a preface to criticism.

## The question we are asking you to examine

Our case, set out in full elsewhere on this site, is that English football's
principal remaining limitation is not talent or mentality but collective
fluency: the trained ability of players developed inside very different club
tactical systems to combine quickly and reliably under pressure. We think
the Premier League gives English players an unusually wide range of tactical
experience — and that nothing in the national framework currently guarantees
that range is converted into a shared method rather than left as a source of
friction.

We are not certain this is right. We are asking you to find out.

Specifically, we ask the FA to commission and publish an honest internal
audit of interoperability across England's age groups: whether under-15,
under-18, under-21 and senior squads share genuine common language for
tactical situations; whether game-state preparation — how a team recognises
and responds to specific match situations — is trained consistently as
players move between age groups; and whether the current framework
translates well between the different club systems your players actually
arrive from, or quietly asks them to set that experience aside.

## What we are asking for

We ask you to examine this question with more access and more rigour than
an outside project can bring, and to publish what you find — including if
the answer is that the current framework already does this well. A published
finding either way would be more valuable to English football than a
private one, and more valuable than the guesswork that fills the gap when
nobody outside the organisation can see the answer.

We make this case in good faith, in public, and we would welcome the chance
to be told where we have it wrong.
