## Written for the FA, not against it

The twelve proposals that follow are addressed to the Football Association
in good faith. This site is not affiliated with the FA and has no inside
knowledge of its current plans, but it takes seriously that the FA has
already built real institutional capacity — St George's Park, England DNA,
coach education pathways — that any credible proposal has to work with,
not around <Cite id="fa-st-georges-park" /> <Cite id="fa-england-dna-updates" />.
None of what follows assumes incompetence, bad faith, or a governing body
that needs to be shamed into action. It assumes an organisation that would
reasonably want to know whether its framework is doing the specific job this
site argues matters most: turning England's tactical diversity into
collective fluency, rather than leaving it as a source of friction.

## Testable and reversible, on purpose

Every proposal here is written to be **testable** — it specifies, in
advance, what evidence would show it working and what would show it not
working — and **reversible**, meaning it can be scaled back or abandoned
without structural damage if the evidence does not support it. This is a
deliberate constraint, not a hedge. A policy proposal that cannot fail
gracefully is a proposal that will be defended past the point the evidence
justifies, and English football has enough of those already.

## Why each proposal carries its own objection

Each of the twelve proposals is presented with five fixed elements: what it
is, how it would be implemented, who inside the game would own it, the
strongest objection to it, and how we would respond to that objection. Two
further elements sit alongside these: a stated measure of success, and an
honest account of the unintended consequences it could plausibly cause.

We include the objection and the unintended-risk sections deliberately, and
we write them as the strongest version of the counter-case we can manage,
not a weak version included for appearances. A policy proposal that only
ever states its own case is not making an argument — it is making an
advertisement. Readers, including anyone inside the FA weighing whether any
of this is worth pursuing, deserve to see the trade-offs stated plainly
enough to disagree with intelligently.

<Callout tone="note" title="What this list is not">
  This is not a blueprint that must be adopted in full or not at all. Some
  proposals will be more defensible than others; some may already be partially
  in motion inside the FA in forms this site has no visibility into. Treat each
  one on its own terms — as a specific, falsifiable idea worth testing, not as a
  single package to accept or reject wholesale.
</Callout>

## What ties them together

Every proposal on this list exists to serve the same underlying goal set out
in <a href="/the-model/">the model</a>: standardising the shared language,
recognition and response layer of England's football, while deliberately
leaving style, formation and philosophy alone. None of them asks the FA to
copy another country's system. All of them ask a narrower, more answerable
question: does the current framework give England's players — developed
inside the most tactically plural elite league in the world — a genuine
shared method for playing together, and if not, what specific, testable
change would move it closer?
