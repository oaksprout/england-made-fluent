## What a source entry contains

Every entry in the bibliography records the same fields: a title, the
organisation or author responsible for it, a publication date where known, a
URL, the date we accessed it, a source type, the countries and eras it
relates to, the specific claims it is being used to support, notes on what
still needs verifying, and a verification status. That last field is the
one that matters most.

## Source types

Sources are classified as **official** (federation, league or governing-body
material), **academic** (peer-reviewed or scholarly literature), **data**
(structured statistical datasets or technical reports), **journalism**,
**book**, **interview**, or **historical** (accounts of events predating
routinely archived digital sourcing). The type is shown alongside every
citation so readers can judge how much weight a given claim should carry —
official federation material and peer-reviewed research are not the same
kind of evidence as a single piece of journalism, even when both are
accurately cited.

## Publication date versus accessed date

Where a source has a clear original publication date, we record it
separately from the date we accessed the material. This matters for
anything time-sensitive: a Premier League academy statistic, a federation
policy document, or a data point that could have been superseded since. The
accessed date tells a reader how current our reference point was, even when
the underlying publication date is older or unclear.

## Verification status, and what each status actually means

Every source carries one of three statuses. `placeholder` means we have
identified the organisation or literature that should hold the relevant
material — usually with a specific candidate URL — but neither the
citation's identity nor its content has been sufficiently confirmed.
`corroborated` means the exact citation details (title, author, publisher
or journal, date, URL or DOI) have been confirmed against multiple
independent bibliographic and search records, but the document itself has
not yet been retrieved and read in full against the claim it supports.
`verified` — which no source has yet reached — means a researcher has
retrieved and read the material and checked it directly against the claim.

These statuses are not minor caveats. Until an entry reaches `verified`, do
not treat any date, figure, or characterisation of a source's contents on
this site as confirmed. The `<Cite>` marker used throughout the site visibly
flags non-verified sources wherever they appear, rather than hiding the
distinction in fine print.

## How sources are prioritised

When verifying or extending the bibliography, priority runs from official
federation and governing-body material (FA, UEFA, FIFA, RFEF, DFB, FFF, FIGC,
KNVB, CBF, AFA, HNS, Premier League), to peer-reviewed academic research, to
credible sports historians and analysts, to reputable data journalism and
statistical services. General-interest journalism and secondary
summarisation are used sparingly and flagged as such, never as a substitute
for a primary source that is realistically obtainable.

## Contested interpretations

Some readings of football history and tactics are genuinely disputed among
serious analysts and historians — for example, how much credit a specific
academy or reform programme deserves for a subsequent tournament result.
Where that is the case, the relevant `<Callout>` is marked
`tone="contested"` rather than `tone="interpretation"`, signalling that
credible people disagree, not just that this is our own reading of settled
facts.

## Correction policy

If a source's verification status is wrong, if a citation does not actually
support the claim it is attached to, or if you have located material that
should update a placeholder entry, please contact

<a href={`mailto:${"info@gtawyb.com"}`}>info@gtawyb.com</a> with the source ID,
the page it appears on, and what you believe should change. Corrections will be
reflected in the bibliography directly rather than argued away.
